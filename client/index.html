How to Use BuildCalc
1
Payments & Scheduling
Critical-path project schedule and contractor payments, driven by the Estimator

Construction Phases aren't typed in by hand — every item you've costed out in the Project Estimator becomes its own phase here automatically, with the dollar amount pulled live from the Estimator. Overhead and Soft Costs live entirely in the Estimator now, so this tab is just Construction Phases and scheduling.

  1. Set a project name at the top.
  2. Construction Phases populate on their own from whatever's been costed in the Project Estimator — every costed item gets its own phase, so items in the same CSI division (like Roofing and Insulation, both under Thermal & Moisture) can still run on completely different schedules with different subs. Add or change a cost in the Estimator, and the matching phase (and its dollar figure) updates here — nothing to enter twice. Click a phase's name or Total to see the line item behind it (click Open in Estimator from there to jump to its division), or drag the row by its number to reorder phases however you like.
  3. Need several items scheduled together instead (same sub, same dates)? In a phase's drill-down, use the Phase dropdown on any item to group it into a named custom phase — it can pull items from more than one division. Set it back to — Its own phase — to split it out again. Rename or delete a custom phase from its row the same way as any other. Click All Items in the top bar to see and re-group every item in one list, instead of opening each phase one at a time.
  4. Use the Status dropdown per phase to track progress: Not Started → Bid Needed → In Progress → Complete.
  5. Set a Start and End date per phase to build your schedule — once both are set for at least one phase, a colored Schedule timeline appears below the table (click its header to expand/collapse).
  6. On that timeline, drag the middle of a phase's bar to reschedule it (both dates shift together, duration preserved), or drag either edge to resize just that end. A small tooltip follows the bar showing exactly what date(s) you're on. Snaps to whole days and saves automatically.
  7. The Notes field is one line, so long notes can get cut off — click the small expand icon next to it to read or edit the full note in a larger box.
  8. Click a phase's Payments button to assign a subcontractor and log payments against it — see phase total, paid, and remaining balance, plus full payment history. This tracks who's owed what; it doesn't move real money. If a subcontractor's actual quote differs from the Estimator's number, enter it as the Contract Amount — that becomes the number used for payments, progress, and the summary panel on this tab, while the original Estimator estimate stays untouched and visible for comparison. Use Print Receipt for a printable payment record, or Copy Share Link to give the subcontractor a no-login link where they can check their own balance and payment history.
  9. Any Soft Cost you've entered on the Estimator (permit fees, insurance, architect, etc.) gets its own row here too, in a separate section below Construction Phases, once it's above $0. Click its Payments button to log real payments against it the same way as a phase — there's no Status, Schedule, or Contract Amount for these (the amount always comes straight from the Estimator), just payment tracking and an optional note.
  10. Click Subcontractors in the top bar to manage your subcontractor list — it's shared across every project, so add one once and reuse it anywhere.
  11. The summary panel's Custom section breaks your budget down the same way the Estimator does: Direct Cost (your Construction Phases, using each phase's real Contract Amount where you've entered one) plus Soft Cost (permits, insurance, architect, loan interest, etc.) add up to Total Budget — the real cost of the job, with no markup. Total Profit is the Estimator's Profit % applied to Total Budget, and Overhead (the Estimator's Overhead %) is shown right below it. Estimator Bid Price is the full number you'd actually quote a client — Total Budget plus Overhead plus Total Profit — updated live as Contract Amounts come in. Click it to jump to the Estimator.
  12. Once at least one payment's been logged (to a sub or a Soft Cost item), the summary panel also breaks down how much of the job is behind you: Profit Taken is the Estimator's Profit % applied to everything paid so far — Paid to Subs plus Paid for Soft Costs combined — with Profit Remaining (Total Profit minus Profit Taken) below it. Payments Remaining (Direct Cost minus Paid to Subs) sits under Paid to Subs once at least one sub payment's logged, and Soft Costs Remaining sits under Paid for Soft Costs the same way once a Soft Cost payment's logged — each tracked independently.
  13. The Spec section below that is for spec builds sold at a market price instead of a fixed client bid. Enter your Land Cost and expected Sales Price — Realtor Fee and Title Insurance default to 3% and 1% of Sales Price but can be changed. Spec Profit is Sales Price minus Land Cost, Realtor Fee, Title Insurance, and Total Budget (from the Custom section above), shown alongside its margin as a percentage of Total Budget plus Land Cost.
  14. Click Export PDF for a full closeout report: every Construction Phase with its status, schedule, subcontractor, and paid/remaining; every Soft Cost the same way; the full financial summary (Direct Cost, Soft Cost, Total Budget, Overhead, Total Profit, Estimator Bid Price, and whatever's been paid so far); and the Spec Build breakdown if you've used it.
2
Blueprint Takeoff
Measure directly on uploaded PDFs and drawings

Upload a PDF or image of a drawing and measure linear footage, square footage, and counts directly on screen. Measurements push straight into the Project Estimator.

  1. Click Open (or drag & drop) to load a PDF or image drawing. The file syncs to your account (up to 20MB), so opening the project on a different computer pulls down the same drawing automatically — watch the small status next to the file name for "Syncing…", "✓ synced", or a warning if it's too large. If it has a printed scale note (e.g. 1/4" = 1'-0"), BuildCalc tries to read it automatically — from the file's text if it's a digital export, or via OCR if it's a scanned drawing — and shows a Scale Detected popup for you to confirm before it's used. Nothing found? Set it manually the same way as always.
  2. Click Set Scale, click two known points on the drawing, then enter their real-world distance in feet. Use the +/− zoom controls to scale the view, or hold Shift and drag to pan, or hold Shift and scroll to zoom, at any time.
  3. The Conditions panel (left) starts with 5 common conditions. Click + Library to search and add more from a categorized list, or + New for a fully custom one. Click the pencil icon on any condition to rename it or change its type and color later, or drag conditions to reorder them.
  4. Click a condition to select it, then draw directly on the drawing. Linear: click points, double-click to finish. Area: click corners, double-click to close. Count: single click per item. Hover over any marked-up shape to see which condition it belongs to.
  5. Already have a matching measurement elsewhere (e.g. Slab and Hardwood Floors cover the same area)? Click the copy icon next to a condition's Send to Estimator button to copy another condition's value straight in — no re-drawing needed. Works across every page of the file, not just the one you're on.
  6. Click the clear icon next to a condition to wipe every measurement for just that condition, on every page — it asks for confirmation first, and the condition itself stays (only its markup is removed). Click the ✕ icon instead to delete the condition outright, along with its measurements and any Estimator line item it was pushed to — this also asks for confirmation first.
  7. Use Hide Layer to click and hide a layer on the drawing — click a hidden condition again in the Conditions panel to bring it back — or Erase to click and delete a single measurement.
  8. Ready to send just one condition? Click its Send to Estimator button to pick a CSI division, description, and unit cost for that condition alone. To do them all at once, use Push All to Estimator to assign CSI divisions and unit costs for every measured condition, then send to the Project Estimator. Conditions with nothing measured yet are skipped automatically, and totals combine across every page a condition was marked up on.
  9. Use the Undo/Redo buttons (or Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z) to step back or forward through any change — measurements, conditions, or reordering, on any page. Backspace removes the last point while drawing, Escape cancels.
3
Project Estimator
Detailed line-item estimating by CSI MasterFormat division

Build a detailed bottom-up estimate organized by construction division, using your own real numbers throughout. Markup is applied on top to produce a final bid price.

  1. Set your project name in the top bar.
  2. Click a CSI division in the left panel (e.g. 03 Concrete) to open it, or click See All at the top of that list to view every division's items together in one table. Hover a division and click the pencil icon to rename it to whatever you want — the new name shows everywhere that division appears, including its matching phase in Payments & Scheduling.
  3. Need a division the CSI list doesn't cover? Click Add Division at the bottom of the left panel to create your own. Custom divisions work exactly like the standard ones — click the trash icon next to one to delete it (this also deletes any items still in it, after confirming).
  4. Click + Custom to add a blank row and enter your own description, unit, and cost (needs a specific division selected, not See All). Click the ✕ next to any item to remove it.
  5. Enter quantities for each item — extended costs update instantly. Drag an item's row to reorder it within its division, or drop it onto a row from a different division to move it there.
  6. Soft Costs, at the top of the right panel, covers real fixed dollar costs that don't scale with the size of the job: Permit Fees, bank loan interest, insurance, architect/designer, geotech/survey, structural engineer, and project consultant. Enter a dollar amount for whichever apply — leave the rest blank — and they roll up to a Total Soft Costs line.
  7. Markup & Fees covers Overhead % and Profit %, both applied to Direct Cost plus Total Soft Costs combined, rolling up to a Total Markup & Fees line.
  8. Click Export PDF to generate a full bid document broken out by division, with your markup and soft costs applied.

Tip: every time you click Save, a version of the whole project is kept. Open the project dropdown at the top and choose Version History to review or restore an earlier save.

Tip: click Share in the project dropdown and enter a client's email to give them a read-only budget summary — they'll see it after logging into their own Client account.
